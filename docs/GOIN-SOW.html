3. Problem Statement & Objective

Problem Being AddressedWhat specific problem, gap, or blocker is this Instaward intended to solve?

Students and young professionals in Metro Manila who can no longer attend a concert or event usually resell their tickets through Facebook groups and get paid by direct bank or e-wallet transfer. Buyers risk fake, duplicated or overpriced tickets, and sellers risk buyers who never pay. Neither side has any protection once money is sent.

GOIN is already working on Stellar testnet. A seller lists a ticket with purchase evidence. An administrator reviews it. The buyer pays in USDC into a Soroban contract that holds the payment until the administrator confirms the ticket transfer, then pays the seller or refunds the buyer. The contract rejects any resale price above the original price and any ticket that was already listed. The web app (React, Freighter wallet) and the backend (wallet sign-in, off-chain evidence storage, OCR receipt reading with optional AI extraction) are built and run the full flow.

What still stops real people from using GOIN:

  • A buyer's money can be stuck. Only the administrator can refund. If the seller never starts the transfer and the administrator does not act, the buyer has no way to get the USDC back. A seller also cannot withdraw a listing before it is sold.
  • Testers cannot get started on their own. Test USDC is issued by a team script run from the command line, and the backend is not yet publicly deployed for outside testers [TO VERIFY: confirm no public backend URL exists]. No outside tester can complete the flow without the team's help.
  • Buyers and sellers cannot easily see what happened. Status is shown per listing, but there is no transaction history, no deadline information, and the new interface is not yet merged and released.
  • It has not been tested by outside users. All runs so far are by the team.
Objective of This InstawardIn one or two sentences, what will be true at the end of 30 days if this Instaward is successful?

In 30 days, GOIN will be a publicly reachable testnet app where a first-time buyer or seller can set up a wallet, get test USDC, and complete a protected ticket sale without the team's help, and where a buyer can recover their own payment if a seller never starts the transfer.

At least 20 external testers recruited through Stellar Philippines will complete full listings and purchases, and every transaction will be public on Stellar Expert.

3.3 Why Stellar & Ecosystem Impact

GOIN needs low fees, fast settlement, a dollar stablecoin and programmable escrow in one place. Stellar provides all four:

  • USDC payments. A ₱2,500 ticket is paid as 44.64 USDC, so neither side carries crypto price risk.
  • Soroban smart contracts. The GOIN contract holds the buyer's USDC and enforces the rules in code: resale price never above the original, one listing per ticket, only the seller can start the transfer, and only the administrator can approve listings or confirm outcomes.
  • Trustlines. Buyers and sellers explicitly opt in to hold the USDC asset.
  • Wallet sign-in (SEP-53). Users sign in to the backend by signing a free message in Freighter, with no password.

The contract Wasm is about 11.5 KB, and each step is a single low-fee testnet transaction. [TO VERIFY: measured fee per step, from Stellar Expert, if the team wants to quote it.]

  • A student who cannot attend. A student bought a ₱2,500 concert ticket but has an exam that day. She uploads her receipt; GOIN reads it and fills in the listing. The administrator approves it, a buyer pays 44.64 USDC into the contract, she transfers the ticket, and the contract pays her once the transfer is confirmed.
  • A buyer worried about being scammed. A buyer pays for a ticket, but the seller never starts the transfer. After the payment deadline, the buyer claims a full refund from the contract without waiting for anyone.

  • A scalper. A seller tries to list a ₱2,500 ticket for ₱4,000, or list the same ticket twice. The contract rejects both.

3.7 How It Works

  1. Set up a wallet. The user connects Freighter on testnet. The in-app checklist funds the account with Friendbot, adds the USDC trustline, and requests capped test USDC from the GOIN faucet.
  2. Sign in. The user signs a free one-time message (SEP-53) so the backend knows which wallet they control. It is not a transaction.
  3. Seller lists a ticket. The seller uploads the receipt. OCR (with optional AI extraction) suggests the event, date, venue, category and price, and flags mismatches. The backend stores the details and evidence off-chain and computes ticket_ref, a SHA-256 hash of the ticket record. The seller signs create_listing; the contract rejects a price above the original or a ticket already listed.
  4. Administrator reviews. The administrator checks the evidence and the receipt-check results, then approves or rejects the listing on-chain.
  5. Buyer pays. The buyer signs purchase. The USDC moves from the buyer into the GOIN contract and a payment deadline starts.
  6. Seller starts the transfer. The seller transfers the ticket through the ticket provider (simulated for this testnet sprint) and signs start_transfer.
  7. Outcome. The administrator confirms success (the contract pays the seller) or failure (the contract refunds the buyer). If the seller never starts the transfer before the deadline, the buyer claims the refund directly.
  8. Verify on-chain. Every step is a Soroban transaction shown in the listing's history with a Stellar Expert link.

3.8 Architecture & Trust Model

This sprint adds three pieces to GOIN's existing architecture: buyer-protection functions in the contract, a public testnet deployment with self-service test USDC, and a clearer interface with on-chain history. GOIN still never holds a user's keys, and the platform operator never holds the escrowed USDC: the contract does.

Application layer. A React + TypeScript web app (Vite) using the Freighter wallet for every signature and a generated typed client for the contract. It reads listing state directly from the contract and transaction history from Stellar.

Server layer. A Node.js (Express) backend with SQLite that keeps everything that must stay off-chain: event details, order numbers and purchase evidence. It provides SEP-53 wallet sign-in, receipt OCR (Tesseract, running locally) with optional AI extraction of text only, and draft-to-listing linking that is checked against the chain. Evidence files and order numbers are visible only to the seller and the administrator. The new testnet faucet sends a capped amount of test USDC per wallet. The backend holds no user funds and cannot move escrowed USDC.

Ledger layer. The GOIN Soroban contract holds each buyer's payment and enforces the price cap, duplicate block, status order and caller permissions. On-chain it stores only ticket_ref, prices, Stellar addresses and status. Personal information, ticket files, QR codes and receipts stay off-chain.

Limitations, explicit by design:

  • The administrator is trusted. One administrator key approves listings and decides whether a transfer succeeded, which decides who receives the escrowed USDC. The buyer-claimable refund removes the administrator only from the "seller never started" case.
  • Ticket transfer is simulated. No ticket provider is integrated. The administrator's confirmation stands in for the provider's confirmation.
  • Review is not proof of authenticity. Approval and receipt checks show that the evidence looked consistent; they do not prove a ticket is real, owned by the seller or transferable. OCR and AI only suggest and flag.
  • Test assets only. The USDC used is a test asset issued by the team on testnet.
  • Price conversion is off-chain. The contract compares prices in USDC base units; the app and backend convert pesos at a fixed demo rate of ₱56 per USDC (₱2,500 = 44.64 USDC). There is no live rate feed.

Roadmap items outside this sprint: ticket-provider integration, multi-party or dispute-based confirmation, organizer dashboard, managed database and private cloud evidence storage at production scale, and mainnet.

7.1 Risks & Mitigations

7.2 Security Controls

  • Non-custodial keys: users sign every action in their own Freighter wallet; GOIN never holds user keys.
  • Contract-held escrow: the buyer's USDC is held by the GOIN contract, not by the seller or the team, and moves only through confirm_transfer, claim_refund or the refund path.
  • Rules enforced on-chain: price cap, duplicate block, status order, seller cannot buy own listing, and caller checks on every function.
  • Minimal on-chain data: only a hash of the ticket record, prices, addresses and status.
  • Backend controls: SEP-53 wallet sign-in with hashed session tokens and expiry, file-type checks on uploads, chain-checked draft linking, seller/administrator-only access to evidence, and rate limits.
  • Testnet only: all testing, validation and transactions in this award are on Stellar testnet with a test USDC asset.
